excerpt: 'Does deskilling cause unrest? I study this question in the context of the First Industrial Revolution in England, using parish-level data on the adoption of skill-saving machines drawn from a corpus of newspaper advertisements. Reduced-form analysis leveraging an event study research design finds a positive causal effect of skill-saving mechanisation on unrest intensity between 1760 and 1814. Heterogeneity analysis of peaceful collective bargaining and petitioning by employers and workers identifies the weakening of labour market institutions as a plausible mechanism, attributable to the imbalance of lobbying power between winners and losers of disruptive technological change.'
