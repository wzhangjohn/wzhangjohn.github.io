excerpt: 'Does elite conflict cause franchise extension? I study this question in the context of the 1832 Great Reform Act in England, using surviving poll books and parish records on the formation of disenfranchising select vestries. Reduced-form analysis leveraging a difference-in-differences research design finds that political elites residing in parishes with democratic backsliding increased their electoral support for the reformist Whig party between the 1830 and 1831 general elections. I attribute the research findings to the outbreak of the Swing Riots, which occurred with greater intensity in select vestry parishes, providing an elite-conflict interpretation of the theory of revolutionary threat.'
